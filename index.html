Which statistical test should I use?

A quick starting guide. Your design, sample and data always decide the final choice, so check it with your supervisor.

Independent t-test

For a numeric outcome in two separate groups, such as scores for two teaching methods.

  • Check roughly normal data and similar spread
  • Not normal or ordinal? Use the Mann-Whitney U test
  • Same people measured twice? Use a paired t-test
One-way ANOVA

For a numeric outcome across three or more groups, such as results at three hospitals.

  • Check normality and equal variances
  • Not normal? Use the Kruskal-Wallis test
  • A significant result needs a post-hoc test to see which groups differ
Pearson correlation

For the strength of a straight-line link between two numeric variables, such as study hours and marks.

  • Check the scatter plot looks roughly linear
  • Ordinal or skewed data? Use Spearman's correlation
  • Correlation alone does not show cause
Chi-square test

For an association between two categorical variables, such as gender and compliance (yes/no).

  • Expected counts in each cell should generally be 5 or more
  • Small counts? Use Fisher's exact test
  • Report an effect size such as Cramer's V
